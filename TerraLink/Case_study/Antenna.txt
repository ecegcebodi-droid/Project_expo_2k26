TerraLink – Final Antenna, Power and Relay Distance Justification

1. Antenna Selection

- TerraLink uses the SX1278 LoRa module at 433 MHz.
- The wavelength at 433 MHz is approximately 69.3 cm.
- A quarter-wave antenna is approximately 17.3 cm long.
- Therefore, a ~17 cm 433 MHz antenna is selected as the theoretical starting point.
- The final antenna should be properly tuned according to the module, ground plane, enclosure and mounting conditions.
- A compact omnidirectional antenna is suitable for the portable TerraLink device.
- Relay nodes and the rescue station can use properly tuned external antennas for better coverage.

2. Antenna Placement

- The antenna should preferably be mounted vertically.
- The portable-node antenna should be kept away from large metal objects.
- Relay-node antennas should be mounted at an elevated position.
- Suitable locations include poles, rooftops, towers or elevated terrain.
- Elevation improves line of sight and reduces obstruction from buildings, trees and terrain.
- The antenna should have sufficient clearance from the electronics and metal structures.

3. Transmit Power

- Transmit power is selected based on range, reliability and battery consumption.
- A prototype target of approximately 17–20 dBm can be considered, depending on the exact SX1278 module and applicable regulations.
- Higher transmit power can improve link performance but increases battery consumption.
- TerraLink uses relay nodes, so each node does not need to transmit across the entire communication distance.
- Moderate transmit power therefore provides a balance between communication reliability and battery life.

4. Relay Node Distance

- The 25 km target represents the overall communication coverage, not necessarily a single 25 km LoRa link.
- TerraLink uses multi-hop communication to divide the total distance into shorter links.
- Example:
  User → Relay 1 → Relay 2 → Relay 3 → Rescue Station
- Relay nodes should be placed according to the reliably tested communication range.
- A safety margin should be maintained instead of placing nodes at the theoretical maximum range.
- For example, if testing provides a reliable range of around 5 km, an initial relay spacing of approximately 2.5–4 km can be considered, depending on the environment.

5. Factors Used to Decide Relay Distance

- Antenna height.
- Antenna gain and efficiency.
- Transmit power.
- Receiver sensitivity.
- LoRa spreading factor.
- Bandwidth.
- Terrain.
- Trees and vegetation.
- Buildings and other obstacles.
- Line-of-sight conditions.
- Radio interference.

6. Relay Node Power

- Relay nodes use rechargeable batteries.
- Major power-consuming components include the SX1278, microcontroller, sensors, display and GPS when active.
- Low-power modes can be used when there is no emergency communication.
- During an SOS event, the node can:
  Wake → Receive → Process → Forward → Return to Low Power
- For long-term deployment, a solar panel + charge controller + rechargeable battery can be used.

7. Overall Justification

- Antenna size: ~17 cm theoretical quarter-wave antenna for 433 MHz.
- Antenna type: Omnidirectional for portable nodes; properly tuned external antennas for fixed nodes.
- Antenna position: Vertical and elevated for improved line of sight.
- Transmit power: Approximately 17–20 dBm as a prototype starting point, subject to regulations.
- Relay distance: Determined by field-tested reliable range with a safety margin.
- Overall coverage: Approximately 25 km through multiple LoRa hops.
- Power source: Rechargeable battery, with solar charging as a future option.
- Main objective: Reliable emergency communication without depending on cellular towers or internet connectivity.

Final Statement

- TerraLink combines proper antenna selection, suitable transmit power, elevated antenna placement, controlled relay spacing and multi-hop LoRa communication.
- This approach provides reliable emergency communication while balancing range, battery life, network reliability and practical deployment requirements.
- The final antenna, power and relay spacing should be validated through real-world field testing before claiming a specific communication range.
